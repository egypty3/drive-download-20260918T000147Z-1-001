Strategic Foundations

Executive Overview: The Business of Ground Handling Contracts

Connecting commercial airline commitments with apron operations and financial revenue assurance.

Aviation Ecosystem Mission-Critical Operations
  • High Operational Velocity: Ground handlers coordinate multi-departmental ramp teams, heavy equipment, and passenger services under tight flight schedules.
  • Global Standardization: Agreements follow recognized international frameworks (such as IATA SGHA) with custom commercial annexes per airline.
  • Airport Station Network: Services span international hubs and regional leisure airports, each with distinct local taxes and authority tariffs.
Commercial Reality Low Margins & High Exposure
  • Tight Profit Margins: Operating margins typically sit between 5% and 12%, leaving little room for billing inaccuracies.
  • Service Leakage Risk: Unlogged ground power extensions, extra baggage tractors, or emergency stairs directly destroy profitability.
  • Airline Audit Rigor: Airlines scrutinize turnaround invoices against contracted tolerance thresholds and dispute unsupported charges.
Application Purpose The Central Contract Engine
  • Operational-Commercial Bridge: Converts real-world physical turnaround events into precise, contractual line items.
  • Automated Billing Rules: Calculates applicable base rates, weight brackets, overtime surcharges, and partner markups.
  • Revenue Protection: Prevents services from being provided on the tarmac without being invoiced.
💡
Core Executive Takeaway: In commercial aviation ground handling, a contract is not a passive legal archive. It is an active calculation engine that transforms physical turnaround timestamps into protected, disputeless revenue.

Contract Master Setup: Commercial Scope & Account Governance

Structuring the baseline commercial rules that govern airline customer relationships across airport stations.

Current System Current Setup Capabilities
  • Carrier Profile & Term Validity: Records airline identities, valid date windows, credit terms, and payment allowances (e.g., 14-day credit).
  • Station & Flight Scope: Defines authorized airport locations and classifies operations by flight type (Charter, Scheduled, Cargo, Technical stops).
  • Aircraft Categorization: Configures baseline turnaround allowances distinguishing narrow-body from wide-body aircraft (e.g., 60 vs. 90 minutes).
  • Mandatory Partner Flags: Captures equipment leasing conditions, fuel supplier mandates, and commercial broker commission scales.

Station additions require repetitive manual re-entry; contract revisions overwrite active terms with limited historical audit trails; expiry dates must be monitored manually.

  • Template-Based Contract Creation: Onboard new airline carriers in minutes by inheriting standard airport handling agreements with tailored overrides.
  • Proactive Expiry & Renewal Alerts: Automated 90-, 60-, and 30-day notifications alerting commercial directors before contracts lapse.
  • Multi-Tier Approval Matrix: Enforces commercial review workflows before custom discount levels or non-standard credit terms take effect.
BUSINESS VALUE

Eliminates unauthorized contractual commitments, accelerates contract rollout by 70%, and ensures complete legal audit readiness.

Current Execution Multi-Layered Rule Framework
  • Minimum Revenue Guarantees: Establishes floor prices ensuring low-load flights still cover baseline turnaround costs.
  • Time Tolerance Gradients: Computes standard included ground windows (e.g., first 2 hours free) and triggers rounded half-hour penalties thereafter.
  • Airport Authority Disclosures: Applies mandatory civil aviation pass-through fees and administrative handling percentages.
  • Opaque Calculation Outcomes: Finance teams cannot easily see the exact arithmetic behind the final invoice number.
New Version Transparent Financial Modeling
  • Formula Inspector: One-click interactive transparency showing the exact equation behind every charge: [Base] + [Overtime GPU] + [5% Fee] = Total.
  • Live Central Bank Exchange Feeds: Locks currency exchange rates on the exact flight operation date, eliminating FX loss.
  • Automated Volume Rebates: Automatically checks cumulative monthly flight counts and applies tiered commercial discounts when milestones are reached.
  • "What-If" Rate Simulator: Allows commercial managers to test proposed airline rate cards against historical flight data to preview margin impact.

Subcontractor Management: Safeguarding Third-Party Margins

Managing specialized vendors to prevent margin erosion across into-plane services.

Commercial Role The Handling Spread Model

Handlers frequently subcontract fuel hydrants, heavy cargo main-deck loaders, and catering. Profitability relies strictly on the positive spread between customer revenue and vendor expense.

Operational Vulnerability Silent Margin Leakage
  • Manual Invoice Matching: Checking third-party supplier bills against flight turnaround sheets is conducted manually weeks after flight completion.
  • Unannounced Cost Increases: If a vendor silently raises airport surcharges, handling margins can turn negative without immediate detection.
  • Disputed Disbursements: Inability to provide proof of third-party delivery leads airlines to withhold payment on disbursement fees.
New Version Automated Margin Shield
  • Automated 3-Way Matching: Instantly cross-checks the Purchase Authorization ➔ Ramp Delivery Log ➔ Vendor Invoice.
  • Negative Margin Alert: Real-time system warning halts quote submission if customer pricing falls below vendor cost thresholds.
  • Vendor Performance Scorecards: Tracks third-party on-time arrival, equipment reliability, and service quality against agreed SLAs.

Commercial Frontline

Quotations, Work Orders & Proforma Invoicing: The Commercial Pipeline

Streamlining ad-hoc charter requests, operational handovers, and final settlement documents.

Stage 1: Pre-Flight Price Quotation & Proforma
  • Upfront Price Certainty: Essential for non-scheduled charters and cash-basis airline operations requiring advance cost clearance.
  • Legal & Regulatory Clauses: Embeds liability disclaimers, local civil aviation taxes, and bank settlement instructions.
  • Credit Verification: Automatically checks the carrier's approved payment terms before dispatch authorization.
Stage 2: Operations Apron Turnaround Work Order
  • Direct Ramp Instructions: Translates commercial agreements into an actionable service checklist for ground handling supervisors.
  • Pre-Approved Tolerances: Explicitly states contracted equipment limits (e.g., maximum 2 hours GPU; 1 set of passenger stairs).
  • Special Handling Directives: Highlights VIP protocols, hazardous cargo stowage, or priority baggage offloading rules.
Stage 3: Settlement Verified Final Tax Invoice
  • Post-Departure Reconciliation: Replaces estimated pre-flight quotes with verified operational turnaround actuals.
  • Deviation Disclosure: Highlights additional services requested by airline flight crew on the ramp with timestamped proof.
  • Immediate Accounting Posting: Transmits approved invoice records to corporate ERP and accounting platforms.
CURRENT LIMITATIONS

Quotations and work orders are rendered as static documents; internal approvals circulate via offline email threads; revisions require manual document regeneration.

NEW VERSION ENHANCEMENTS

Interactive digital quotes with one-click airline acceptance links, automated quote-to-work-order conversion, and side-by-side variance analysis showing Quoted vs. Delivered.

Operational Diagnostic

The Current End-to-End Workflow & Operational Bottlenecks

Tracing the turnaround lifecycle to identify where friction, delay, and financial leakage occur.

Phase 1: Contract Setup & Tariff Maintenance
Risk: High Manual Effort

Commercial staff manually record airline rate sheets, aircraft weight matrices, and airport rules. Overwriting previous seasonal rules without version history creates audit vulnerability.

Phase 2: Pre-Flight Commercial Preparation
Risk: Delayed Response

Commercial teams manually issue quotes and proformas for incoming charter flights. Disconnection from real-time airport slot feeds causes delays in quoting ad-hoc flights.

Phase 3: Apron Execution & Service Delivery
Risk: Unbilled Ramp Services

Ramp crews deliver 20+ operations under extreme time pressure. Services are noted on paper clipboards; ad-hoc captain requests often go unrecorded and never reach finance.

Phase 4: Post-Flight Audit & Final Invoicing
Risk: 3 to 7 Day Invoicing Delay

Billing specialists manually cross-reference paper tally sheets against contract rules. The resulting time lag delays invoice issuance, slows cash collection, and invites airline payment disputes.
